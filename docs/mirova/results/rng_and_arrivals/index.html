Seeds, Streams and Arrivals

An audit of how randomness enters the model, what it was doing wrong, and what changed. Prompted by run-to-run variation that reached a coefficient of variation of 60 % on the metrics the calibration is scored against.

3 review steps4 commits2 repositories 126 existing runs re-analysed2 new runs

How randomness enters at all

Each run builds three Mersenne Twisters. generation from the run seed drives arrival times via OdApplier; default from seed + 1 feeds OTS internals; and the scenario built its own from the run seed again, for desired speed, the IDM speed-adherence factor and route choice.

Randomness enters at exactly four points: arrival headways, desired speed from an empirical distribution, the per-vehicle FSPEED factor N(1.031, 0.1), and route choice. The driver parameters are entirely deterministic — T, a, b and s0 are identical for every car and for every truck. The only driver heterogeneity in the model is desired speed.

One suspicion did not survive checking: the overridden getParameters() in ScenarioGenerator looked as though it discarded the FSPEED draw, but getDefaultParameters() calls getCarFollowingParameters(), which draws afresh per vehicle.

STEP 0

Measure the CRN break

done

Paired campaign comparisons rest on the claim that identical seeds make cells directly comparable, so only the model differs. Testing that required no new runs: the settled campaign's cells differ, at equal headway, only in the relaxation damping — a pure behaviour parameter — at identical seeds.

Answer: yes, Common Random Numbers were broken. The aggregate inflow was bit-identical: 20049 against 20049 vehicles, truck share 15.1978 % against 15.1978 %. The generation stream itself did not diverge. What diverged was the microscopic assignment — which vehicle is a truck, at which point in the stream — and that is what decides whether a breakdown triggers.

Not demonstrated

This did not show that the stream duplication caused it. A second mechanism explains the same pattern: under congestion the generators queue too, and the order in which mainline and ramp vehicles are actually placed shifts. That would happen with fully independent streams and is physical, not a defect. Separating the two needed the same measurement repeated after the fix — which is why the baseline had to be taken first.

06e38078b

A behaviour stream disjoint from arrivals

All three scenarios built their behaviour stream as new MersenneTwister(seed) — the same seed OdApplier's generation stream uses. Two separate generators emitting one identical sequence. The behaviour seed now goes through a SplitMix64 finalizer so the roles start at unrelated points.

75a812777

Replication seeds spaced by a prime

Seeds ran as consecutive integers, so replication k's default stream was bit-identical to replication k+1's arrival stream. The seven replications of a cell were not independent draws, which is exactly what the paired statistics assume. Spacing is now 1000003. Replications 1 and up carry new seeds, so existing campaigns cannot be extended with runs from this revision.

50ed4f994

Keep the derived seed positive

SplitMix64 returns negative values for half of all inputs and jstats rejects any seed at or below zero. Base seed 42 landed negative, so every scenario threw at stream construction. Caught by the verification code, not by the earlier smoke run, whose output was still in the pipe buffer when it was killed.

Verified: behaviour stream matches neither the arrival stream nor default, and default(k) no longer reproduces generation(k+1). A reference run completes to t = 3590/3600 s.

L'Ecuyer jump-ahead substreams were considered and not introduced: jstats' MersenneTwister exposes no jump-ahead, and swapping the generator would reach across all of OTS. Against a period of 219937−1 and some 107 draws per run, seed separation is proportionate. Recorded in the commit.

STEP 2

Ramp geometry and the signal cycle

partly conclusive

Geometry. The modelled ramp runs some 310 m of chord from R7_1 to the merge point (143 m to the curve end, 165 m onward), with a 199 m merge section on L4a. Field studies still resolve platoon structure at 230, 450 and 700 m downstream of a stop line, so a platoon would arrive largely intact. Open: R7_1 is the model boundary, not demonstrably the signalized intersection. If the signal sits further upstream, the distance is longer and the argument weaker.

Periodicity. Sharper limitation than the review assumed: at 1-minute sampling the Nyquist period is 120 s, so the entire 60–120 s band of typical cycle times is unresolvable, not merely its lower edge.

On 2025-10-27 the alternation holds under every detrending and the periodogram peaks at exactly 120 s. On 2025-09-22 there is no alternation under any. So the day with the longest jam at the lowest demand shows a two-minute periodicity in ramp arrivals; the jam-free day does not.

What it does not prove. 120 s sits exactly at Nyquist, which is equally consistent with a 120 s cycle and with an alias of a shorter one. Neither can be excluded; signal timing plans would be the next source. The weaker peaks at 151 s and 181 s on other days are not counted — no correction was made for the number of frequencies examined, and across some 50 bins a periodogram maximum routinely exceeds three standard deviations.

The three items the review left open

done
21735a7e2

Arrival process, per inflow

Mainline: implemented. A shifted exponential m + (1−m)·(−ln u) set at origin node N1_1, enabled by -Dmirova.mainlineHeadwayFloor. Over 2 million draws the mean holds at 1.000 for every m, so OD demand is delivered unchanged, while the floor is exact.

On-ramp: deliberately not implemented, for a structural reason found while building it — a HeadwayDistribution receives only a random stream, never simulation time. A process whose intensity varies with the phase of a signal cycle cannot be expressed through this interface by any distribution. Robertson dispersion needs a time-aware arrivals generator, not a parameter. The ramp keeps the default as an acknowledged approximation, recorded in the Javadoc.

23cbbb8

Confidence intervals on every campaign figure

Student-t half-width and contributing n for each metric; a Wilson score interval for breakdown probability, which is a share of runs and would otherwise leave [0, 1] and collapse to zero width exactly at 0 % and 100 %.

a5283ab

Scoring against the empirical distribution

compare_capacity_distributions puts the simulated product-limit distribution beside the empirical one and names the direction of the error. Checked on synthetic cases: 1.59× reads "too wide", 1.09× "matches", 0.63× "too narrow — overshot". Undershooting the empirical spread counts as a failure, not a better score.

This changes how earlier results should be read

At n = 7 a cell at 57 % breakdown has a Wilson interval of [0.25, 0.84] and one at 86 % has [0.49, 0.97]. The cells were never distinguishable. And the jam duration that looked like the campaign's one clean hit — 69 min against an empirical 70 — is 69 ± 60 min over the four seeds that broke down at all. An earlier estimate of ±13 min, taken from the mean coefficient of variation across cells, understated it considerably.

What remains open

  • The headway floor is untested. The default of 0.0 reproduces current behaviour exactly, so nothing has changed yet. Measuring its effect is a campaign of its own — and given the Wilson intervals it needs more seeds than seven, or the result will again be unresolvable.
  • Ramp platoon arrivals need a time-aware generator. Whether that is worth building depends on whether the 120 s periodicity is real, which 1-minute data cannot settle.
  • The signal's true position relative to R7_1, which decides how much of the platoon survives to the merge.
  • The CRN re-test rests on one pair, across a change of entry point. A clean version would repeat both runs on the pre-commit state through the same entry point.
  • Driver parameters remain deterministic. Adding heterogeneity in T is the obvious next thought and the wrong lever: per Ehrhardt & Tordeux, scaled heterogeneity destabilizes.
