Three Dates, Eighteen Cells

An overnight local campaign crossed two desired-headway pairs with three relaxation-damping levels on three study dates, seven seeds each, to test whether the configuration the single-date grid settled on survives where that grid could not look.

126 runs7 seeds / cell13:00–22:00 window safety distance 0.55local, headless

Result

No cell in this grid is jointly consistent with the site.

Headway and damping move breakdown frequency and smoothness, but the structure of the congestion — too slow, too long, discharging too little — is wrong in all eighteen combinations. Jam speed lands 10–20 km/h below the empirical range in fifteen of them, and on 2025-10-07 every cell runs a jam at least twice as long as the one that was observed. That is a statement about the model physics, not about the parameter choice.

The days do not order by demand

The three dates were picked so that one of them — 2025-09-22 — has no persistent breakdown at all. A configuration made harder to break should be checked first on the day the site did not break.

2025-09-22 4584 veh/h peak

No breakdown observed

2025-10-07 4596 veh/h peak

Breakdown, ~55 min, ~47 km/h

2025-10-27 3840 veh/h peak

Breakdown, ~70 min, ~38 km/h

The date with by far the lowest demand carries the longest jam; the date with higher demand stayed free. The model's breakdown rates, by contrast, order cleanly by demand — 2025-10-07 highest, 2025-10-27 lowest. The model behaves as a demand-driven system should. The observations do not.

A likely cause is in the input, not the model. If the demand series are reconstructed from measured cross-section counts, then on a congested day the count is the discharge, not the demand: the jam caps the measurement. That would explain why the 70-minute day carries the lowest peak in the set. The model is then handed a demand that can no longer produce the jam it is being scored against.

This is a hypothesis, not a measurement — but a decidable one. If the series come from a detector at or downstream of the bottleneck the effect is unavoidable; from a cross-section upstream of the queue it is not. That can be settled from the pipeline's provenance without running a single further simulation.

Why no cell can be called the reliable one

The cell that looked strongest on smoothness and breakdown frequency — 1.10/1.40 at damping 0.95 — does not survive the congestion metrics. It matches the jam duration on 2025-10-27 almost exactly (69 against 70 min) and misses it on 2025-10-07 by a factor of 2.3 (126 against 55). Its jam speed is 14–19 km/h too low on both. And it still produces a 26-minute jam on the date that had none.

The two cells that do land in the empirical jam-speed band reach it the wrong way: 1.20/1.50 at damping 0.90 shows 50.8 km/h on 2025-10-27, but breaks down in only two runs of seven and jams for 22 minutes. The plausible speed comes from there being almost no congestion, not from the congestion being right.

Discharge points the same way. On 2025-10-27 no cell exceeds 2826 veh/h against a pre-breakdown flow of roughly 3300. The model does not have the capacity the site has.

What the campaign did settle

The longer headway is rejected discard

1.20/1.50 breaks down in 86–100 % of runs on the date with no observed breakdown, and at damping 0.90 it also costs 19.7 % stop-and-go and 614 ramp standstills — worse in both target quantities at once. The single-date grid's suspicion that the gradient had not yet flattened at 1.10/1.40 is not confirmed in the direction hoped for.

Damping off is not supported revisit

The monotonicity that held in every headway row of the single-date grid breaks here. On 2025-09-22, damping 0.95 beats 1.00 in both smoothness measures (5.9 against 11.1 % stop-and-go, 161 against 278 standstills); on 2025-10-27 the ordering is inconsistent again. Switching the mechanism off rested on one date and does not carry across three.

The standstill constraint is held holds

Ramp standstills run at 161–858 per run against 1237 in the earlier configuration — lower in every one of the eighteen cells. The requirement that the standstill share must not rise is met throughout, though on 2025-09-22 those standstills occur on a day that should have had none.

Per-day breakdown rate is the wrong target method

As long as the demand series cannot explain why the lowest-demand day carries the longest jam, scoring a deterministic parameter set against the per-day breakdown event asks it to reproduce an ordering that is not in its input. The capacity distribution across days, in Brilon's sense, is the target the existing product-limit and GMM machinery was built for.

Next

  • Trace the provenance of the demand time series before spending further compute on parameter variation. If they are reconstructed from counts at or below the bottleneck, the congested days are systematically under-demanded and every breakdown-rate comparison so far inherits that bias.
  • Jam speed and jam duration are not reachable from the headway–damping plane. They belong to the open items on jam propagation, and need a separate treatment.
  • Record the single mainline cross-section as a validation limitation: the empirical wave structure cannot be resolved from one detector, so the upstream-wave discrepancy is currently unfalsifiable.
